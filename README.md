Water Sensor PCB -- Water Leakage Alarm
A simple water detection alarm designed in KiCad. The circuit
detects the presence of conductive water between two sensing probes and
activates a buzzer to provide an audible warning.
Project status: PCB design files uploaded. Update this section
after assembling and testing the physical circuit.

Overview
Water leaks can go unnoticed and cause damage around sinks, washing
machines, water tanks, and other areas. This project demonstrates a
basic, low-voltage way to detect water and trigger an alarm.

The design is based on the DigiKey Maker project, Water Alarm --
Priceless Protection, Crazy
Simple.
How It Works
The circuit uses two sensing probes:
- When the probes are dry: there is no conductive path between
  them. A pull-down resistor keeps the MOSFET gate low, so the MOSFET
  remains off and the buzzer is silent.
- When water bridges the probes: the water provides a conductive
  path that raises the MOSFET gate voltage. The MOSFET switches on,
  allowing current to flow through the buzzer and produce an alarm.
The water acts as the sensing path; the battery supplies the power for
the buzzer.
Main Components
  Component                           Purpose
  2N7000 N-channel MOSFET             Electronic switch that controls the
buzzer

  10 MΩ resistor                      Pulls the MOSFET gate low when the
probes are dry

  1000 pF capacitor                   Helps protect the MOSFET gate from
voltage spikes

  Buzzer                              Produces the audible alarm

  9 V battery / suitable supply       Powers the circuit
Two conductive probes               Detect the presence of water
Note: Check the buzzer's rated operating voltage and current against
the supply and circuit before assembly. Use the component values and
connections in the final schematic as the source of truth.

Building and Reviewing the Design
1. Clone or download this repository.
2. Open the .kicad_pro file in KiCad.
3. Review the schematic and confirm that it matches the intended
   circuit.
4. Open the PCB layout and check the footprints, board outline, copper
   tracks, and design-rule-check results.
5. Generate Gerber and drill files from the PCB Editor when the board
   is ready for fabrication.
6. Assemble the circuit and test it with the sensing probes first dry
   and then bridged by a small amount of ordinary conductive water.
Safety and Limitations
- This is a low-voltage prototype, not a certified
  flood-protection or safety device.
- Do not connect the sensing probes or circuit to mains electricity.
- Use a suitable battery or isolated low-voltage supply.
- Water conductivity varies. Very pure or distilled water may not
  trigger the circuit reliably.
- Keep the electronics protected from moisture; only the intended
  sensing probes should contact water.
- Prolonged current through water can corrode the probes. Inspect and
  clean them periodically.
- Test the completed circuit under controlled conditions before
  relying on it.
